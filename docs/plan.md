## The one-sentence answer

Your knowledge document asks you to write down, in one sentence, why a rider would open your site instead of asking their WhatsApp group. **Recommended:**

> *It is the link someone pastes into the group when the seasonal question comes up — one page per route, where every fact shows who confirmed it and on what date.*

This means the site does not compete with the group. It is what the group points to. The rider who knows the answer stops retyping it every May.

## What the research says not to build

| Do not build | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A forum, comment threads, or chat | The most successful one-person site in this space shut its forum over spam and upkeep. Three other Indian rider forums are gone, and their addresses now serve gambling content or are for sale. Public asking has fallen by about 80 percent since 2022. | Strong — seen by several researchers |
| Your own "is the pass open today" status | One person cannot originate daily status. The best blogger's pages were 16 and 53 days old. | Strong |
| Route drawing, navigation, booking | Solved by others. Riders say the missing piece is data, not software. | Moderate |
| An AI trip planner | It would inherit the same stale data. No evidence it is what riders lack. | Weak — thin evidence either way |
| Logins, points, badges, at the start | Expect a handful of contributors. Build for five people, not five thousand. | Moderate |
| A phone app | A web page opens from a pasted link. An app does not. | Inference |

## What to build first

### All famous routes, in two levels

**Decided by you, 28 September 2026:** the site covers all the famous routes, not one. My first draft recommended a single route. This section replaces that.

The risk with many routes is that each page ends up thin, and a thin page looks like the tour-operator pages that already crowd the search results. The answer is to go **wide with basic pages and deep on one route**.

| Level | What it holds | Where the facts come from | Needs contributors? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic page** — every route | Map of the route, distance and usual number of days, season window, permits with official links, rules for motorcycles, fuel gaps and the fuel check, altitude of night halts, packing list, pinned videos | Official sources, published distances, riders' public videos | No |
| **Full page** — one route at first | Everything in the basic page, plus: who decides and who announces, season history by year, mechanics, mobile network by halt, real leg timings, stays with no online presence | Trip reports and riders confirming facts | Yes |

A basic page is about twenty facts. A full page is about a hundred. Every fact on either level still shows its source and date.

**A route moves from basic to full when riders start reporting on it.** You do not choose which routes go deep; the trip reports do.

### Every route is on the map from the first day

All fifty appear on the site's map from launch. A route whose page is not written yet says so plainly, and carries one button: **"I have ridden this — send a trip report."**

This turns the unwritten pages into an invitation. It also tells you which routes riders care about, so you write those first.

### What this costs in time

Mountain and border routes need about twenty facts each: permits, altitudes, fuel gaps, season dates. Plains and coastal routes need about twelve. Fifty routes come to roughly 850 facts.

| Way of working | Time per fact | Total for 850 facts |
|---|---|---|
| You find, check and date each fact yourself | About 15 minutes | About 210 hours |
| I gather each fact with its link; you check it | About 5 minutes of your time | About 70 hours |

The full page for Manali to Leh adds about 25 hours. Building the site is on top of that.

At 4 to 6 hours a week you have roughly 85 to 125 hours between November and March. **Working alone, fifty routes do not fit. With me gathering the facts, the first batch fits by March, and the other two follow through 2027.**

**Recommended:** I gather, you check, in batches. Every fact you have not yet checked is marked "not yet checked" on the page. Nothing unchecked is shown as confirmed.

**What the research does not cover:** it looked at the Himalaya and at North-East permits. The other regions were not researched at all. The facts that matter there are different — monsoon closures, forest road timings, ghat road conditions — and need their own look before the second batch.

### What goes on the page

Each section is a small table of facts, not an article.

| Section | What it holds | How often it changes | Exists anywhere today? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who decides, who announces | For each stretch: which body, which account or helpline | Rarely | **No. Found nowhere.** |
| Where to check today | Links to those authorities, each showing the date of their latest update | Links rarely; their content daily | Scattered |
| Season history | Actual opening and closing dates for each pass, each year, with source | Twice a year | Partly, on one blog |
| Rules for motorcycles | Early-season bans on bikes, rental-bike restrictions, permits and fees for two-wheelers, how to pay tolls without a FASTag | About yearly | **No. Official pages address taxis; operators address renters.** |
| Permits and fees | The current rule, the official link, and a dated list of every change | About yearly | No dated change list exists |
| Fuel | Each pump: when it opened, source, date a rider last confirmed it working | About yearly; stock weekly | Map on one blog; stock nowhere |
| Acclimatisation | Sleeping altitude of each usual halt, and a check of your night-by-night plan | Never | Rule exists for people flying in; not for riders climbing from Manali |
| Network | Which operator worked at which halt, and when | Yearly, in large steps | One blog |
| Mechanics and puncture repair | Name, village, phone, what they can fix, date last confirmed | Each season | **No. Anecdote only.** |
| Leg timings | Hours actually taken, by riders, with month and type of bike | Slowly | No |
| Stays with no online presence | Phone, bike parking, date last confirmed | Each season | Partly |

### Every fact carries its own date

This is the core of the design. Each fact shows four things:

1. The fact itself
2. Where it came from — a link, or the name of the rider who reported it
3. The date it was last confirmed
4. Its age, shown plainly: fresh, ageing, or stale

**Recommended ageing limits** (yours to tune):

| Kind of fact | Ageing after | Stale after |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel in stock | 7 days | 21 days |
| Mechanic or stay still operating | 6 months | 14 months |
| Network coverage | 6 months | 12 months |
| Permit rule or fee | 6 months | 12 months |
| Pump exists | 12 months | 24 months |

A stale fact stays on the page, marked stale. Hiding it would destroy the record; showing it as current would be dishonest.

**Evidence:** OpenStreetMap does this with a "check date" on each fact, and re-asks at an interval set by how long that kind of fact usually lasts.

### Contributing without leaving WhatsApp

Beside each fact, two buttons: **"Still true"** and **"This has changed"**.

**Recommended:** each opens WhatsApp with a message already written ("Tandi pump — still working, confirmed today"), addressed to a number you control. You read it, and update the page. No account, no form, no new app.

- Use a **separate number**, not your personal one. It will be public.
- Offer a plain web form as well, for people who prefer it.
- You approve every change by hand at first. At this scale that is minutes a day in season, and it keeps out spam.

**Credit every contributor by name on the fact itself:** "confirmed by Rahul, 14 June 2027". **Evidence:** recognition for editing and reviewing is the kind that measurably increases contribution. Moderate evidence, from one study.

### How it spreads

- Every section and every fact has its own short link.
- A pasted link shows a preview card in WhatsApp that carries the answer: "Tandi pump — confirmed working 14 June 2027".
- **Recommended:** plan for pasted links as the main way people arrive. Treat search as a bonus. **Evidence:** AI summaries now answer question-shaped searches on the results page; clicks roughly halve when one appears.

### How it outlives you

BCMTouring held twenty years of riders' knowledge. Its founder died in 2019; the forum vanished in 2024; the address now serves betting content.

**Recommended:**

- Keep all facts as plain files in a public repository, under an open licence that lets anyone copy them with credit.
- Publish a full download of the data on the site itself.
- Name one other person who holds the keys.

If you stop, the record survives. For a project whose purpose is memory, this matters more than any feature.

## Additions you asked for

Added 28 September 2026, at your request. **This section is judgement, not research** — none of the research tracks covered bikes, gear or videos.

### The rule

Everything hangs from a route, comes from a real trip, and carries a date.

A general list of "best touring bikes" or "top 10 riding gear" competes with large sites and hundreds of video channels. The route version is something nobody has.

### The six additions

| Addition | Do not build | Build instead | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Videos | A list of "best Ladakh vlogs" | Videos pinned to each stretch of the route, each with the date it was filmed, so riders see the road as it was that month | First version |
| Fuel check | — | The rider picks their bike; the page shows where on the route they must carry extra fuel | First version |
| Packing list | A general gear checklist | What to carry on this route in this month | First version, written by you; later corrected by trip reports |
| Bike recommendations | "Best touring bikes in India" | Which bikes riders took on this route, what broke, and how each bike's range compares with the longest fuel gap | Second version |
| Accessory recommendations | "Top 10 riding gear" | What riders carried on this route, what they used, and what failed | Second version |
| Real trip costs | A budget estimate | What riders actually spent, by route and month | Second version |

### Why three of them can go in the first version

- **Videos** cost one link, one stretch and one date each. Riders who make videos want viewers, so featuring them gives them a reason to share your page. That helps with the hardest problem, getting started.
- **The fuel check** needs only two things you will already have: the distance between pumps on the route, and a small table of bikes with tank size and typical mileage. It is a calculation on the page, with no server. On Manali to Leh the longest gap is reported as about 320 kilometres.
- **The packing list** can start as your own list for the route. It needs no contributors to be useful.

### Why the other three wait

Bike recommendations, accessory recommendations and real trip costs are only worth having if they come from real trips. With no trip reports they would be your opinion, and a site of one person's opinions is a blog. They switch on once enough reports exist — **recommended: at least ten reports for a route.**

### One trip report feeds everything

Do not build separate sections that each need filling. Build one short form a rider completes after a trip:

1. Route and month
2. Bike
3. Hours taken for each leg
4. Gear that helped, and gear that failed
5. Fuel stops and mechanics used
6. What the trip cost
7. Video link, if any

That one report updates the bike section, the accessory section, the costs, the videos, the leg timings, and the "last confirmed" dates on the facts.

**Keep the form short.** Every field except route, month and bike should be optional. A rider who fills three fields is worth more than one who abandons a form of twenty.

### Cautions

- **Money and trust.** Links to gear are the obvious way to earn a little. Recommendations must come from trip reports, never from who pays. Any link that earns money must be marked as such. Earnings have not been researched yet.
- **Videos belong to their makers.** Show them through YouTube's own player and credit the rider by name. Do not download or re-upload.
- **Bike figures vary.** Mileage drops at altitude and on bad roads. The fuel check must show a safety margin and say where its numbers came from.
- **Do not launch everything at once.** A large empty site is worse than a small full one.

## How you will know it is working

Hobby-scale measures. No traffic targets.

| Measure | Working | Not working |
|---|---|---|
| Riders other than you who confirmed or corrected a fact, first season | 20 or more | Fewer than 5 |
| Rider-confirmed facts that are not stale, during June | 70 percent or more | Under 40 percent |
| Your time in peak season | Within the hours you chose | Regularly over |
| Note-keepers still active in June | 2 or more | None |
| Trip reports received for the route, first season | 10 or more | Fewer than 3 |

**The honest stop rule:** if fewer than five outside riders contribute in a full season, this is a personal reference site, not a community. That may still be worth keeping. But call it what it is, and do not build community features on top of it.
